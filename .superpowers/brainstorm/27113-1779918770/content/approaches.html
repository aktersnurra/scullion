Three directions for the AI-first redesign

Same underlying data and logic — different answer to "what is this app, really?"

A

The Briefing

Home screen is a daily AI-written note. One paragraph, warm, specific to your week. "You've got a good setup this week — roast chicken tonight cascades into three more meals. Pick up yogurt before Wednesday." Below it: tonight's recipe card, then a compact week strip. No widgets, no stats. The AI's voice is the home screen. NL bar lives at the bottom of every screen. Other screens (week view, grocery list, recipes) stay focused on their job.

Strengths

  • Most "alive" — feels like something is thinking about your week
  • Every open feels fresh and contextual
  • Very low information density = zero overwhelm

Trade-offs

  • Requires LLM call on home screen load (or cached on schedule)
  • If the note is generic or wrong, it feels hollow
B

The Planner-First

Week view is the home screen. AI fills it in automatically on Saturday. You open the app and the plan is already there — just review and nudge. Each slot shows a recipe card with a food photo. Pinning, swapping, skipping all done inline. NL bar at the bottom for anything else. No separate "generate" step — the plan just exists, updated weekly. The AI is invisible infrastructure, not a feature.

Strengths

  • Matches how you actually use it — you want to see the week
  • AI is ambient, not a button you press
  • Closest to the mock you shared

Trade-offs

  • Less "alive" day-to-day — it's the same plan all week
  • Home screen gets stale by Wednesday
C

The Hybrid — Tonight + Week

Home screen has two parts. Top half: tonight, AI-contextualised. "Chicken fried rice — 20 min, uses Sunday's chicken." One line of AI commentary that's actually useful, not decorative. Bottom half: the rest of the week as a compact strip. Tap any day to expand. NL bar always visible. The week view is a separate deeper screen for planning mode. Home is for cooking mode — you're standing in the kitchen, you need now, not the whole plan.

Strengths

  • Serves both contexts: quick daily check-in and planning session
  • AI commentary stays relevant because it's about tonight, not the abstract week
  • Kiosk and phone share the same "tonight" mental model

Trade-offs

  • Two jobs on one screen — needs careful hierarchy to not feel split
